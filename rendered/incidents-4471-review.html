Shared parser reads dates month first

Day-first tenants send a date such as 7 April and receive 4 July. The shared parser treats the month as the first field. This is the dominant cause in #4471. It affects app and email reports, and forms that use booking-v2, across the tenants in this export.

Evidence: R-41200, Harbour Dental, app, en-GB, booking-v2. The report says “Our 7 April reminder went out on 4 July.” R-41201 reverses the example but still describes a month/day swap; its separate report ID is retained.

Reproduce the stated code path with a day-first tenant and a date whose day and month differ and are both at most twelve. Expected: the entered local date survives unchanged. Actual reported behavior: the day and month swap. Fix the shared parser’s date convention and verify app, email and booking-v2 inputs. This is a reproduction derived from the export’s logic and reports; the production application was not available to test.

Booking-v3 prints the wrong date hint

Booking-v3 prints MM/DD/YYYY for day-first tenants while the form reads the tenant’s day-first convention. People follow the hint and their dates swap. This is a separate form problem at Alder Physio, Copper Studio and Pale Harbour.

Evidence: R-41230, Copper Studio, form, es-ES, booking-v3. The report describes 6 January becoming 1 June. Its browser locale is en-GB and intake calls it parser, but the tenant and form put it in the hint group. Changing intake tags would not correct the cause.

Reproduce with Copper Studio’s booking-v3 form. For 6 January, follow the printed hint and enter 01/06/2026. Expected: 6 January. The stated form reader interprets this as 1 June. Make the hint agree with the tenant’s date convention and validate the date round trip. Keep a separate ticket and verify this fix independently of the shared parser.

Receipt versus triage

These are two different clocks: received is when a report arrived; merged is when the desk put it in #4471. The first April reports were merged in July. A merge spike therefore does not mean the product suddenly broke that week. Read both series before describing the incident’s start, growth or response time.

Correcting the merge rule

Matching a subject does not establish a shared cause. Different subjects can describe the same bug, and the same subject can describe different bugs. The original triage page and decision 0001 are old, and their rendered examples contradict the rule.
